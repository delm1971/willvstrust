Estate Planning Checklist: What to Gather Before You Start

A little preparation makes the next step clearer.

Use this as a preparation tool

You do not need to make every legal decision before you organize your information. Start with what you know and keep a separate list of questions. This checklist helps you prepare for a conversation or questionnaire; completing it does not create a legal estate plan. Keep sensitive information in a secure place rather than sending it through an ordinary contact form.

1. List the people who matter

Gather correct names and contact details for family members and people you are considering for responsibilities. Note the questions you want to discuss with them. Would they be comfortable with the role? Do they have time to help? Who could be a backup? Separate your preferences from decisions you have not yet made.

2. Make an inventory you can maintain

List financial accounts, property, insurance, debts, retirement benefits, and professional contacts. Record where supporting documents are stored. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau recommends organizing important financial information and making it accessible to trusted people when needed. Keep account credentials protected and separate from an ordinary planning worksheet. Source: CFPB preparation guidance.

3. Write down questions before selecting documents

  • What instructions have I already recorded?
  • Where are my current signed documents?
  • Which people should I speak with before naming them?
  • Which accounts have beneficiary designations I need to review?
  • What legal questions should I take to an attorney?

4. Think about help during your lifetime

Planning can also address who assists with money or health decisions when you cannot act. A durable financial power of attorney and a health care document serve different purposes. Choosing someone for financial authority deserves care: a trusted contact at a financial institution is not the same as an agent authorized to transact. Ask a professional to explain the scope you are considering.

5. Plan for completion and review

Create a short follow-through list: review the draft, resolve unanswered questions, complete the applicable signing steps, arrange any required asset transfers, and decide where originals will be kept. Put a reminder on your calendar to review the plan after major changes. Store the information so it is useful to the right people without unnecessarily exposing private details.

Ready for the next conversation?

Bring your inventory, current documents, and question list to the service or professional you choose. Avoid rushing through unfamiliar terms just to finish a questionnaire. Preparation gives you a better basis for evaluating whether a DIY process fits your needs.

Common questions

Should I enter account passwords into a public worksheet?

No. Keep credentials secure and separate. This website does not collect your estate planning information.

Does this checklist replace legal documents?

No. It organizes information and questions; it does not create or execute documents.
